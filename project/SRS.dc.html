1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document states what the MediCare+ Hospital Management System must do and the qualities it must have. It is written for the project supervisor and examiner, and for whoever maintains the system after us.

1.2 Scope

The reference facility is the University of Ghana Medical Centre: a teaching hospital with outpatient clinics, a laboratory, imaging, a pharmacy, inpatient wards and an accounts office. Requirements were drawn from interviews and observation as described in §4, and the Centre is the case the system was designed against rather than a client that commissioned it. The system covers one such facility and follows the patient from the front desk to discharge: registration, appointments, nurse triage, consultation, laboratory and imaging orders, pharmacy, ward and bed management, billing, insurance claims, and administration. It has two applications on one shared record:

  • A staff web application for nine roles: patient portal, doctor, nurse, receptionist, laboratory technician, radiologist, pharmacist, cashier and administrator.
  • A patient mobile application for booking, records, notifications, Mobile Money payment and an AI symptom checker.

Out of scope for version 1.0: payroll and HR, procurement, multi-facility operation, medical device feeds such as HL7 or DICOM, and national health information exchange. These appear in the evolution roadmap instead.

1.3 Definitions

MRN is Medical Record Number. EMR is Electronic Medical Record. MAR is Medication Administration Record. STAT means an urgent order. NHIS is the National Health Insurance Scheme. MoMo is Mobile Money.

1.4 Conventions

Functional requirements are numbered FR-nn, non-functional NFR-nn, constraints CON-nn and assumptions ASM-nn. "Shall" means mandatory. Priority follows MoSCoW: Must, Should, Could.

2. Overall description

2.1 Problem context

Most hospitals of this size run on paper folders for notes, a separate book or spreadsheet for billing, another register for pharmacy stock, and word of mouth between departments. Folders go missing. Laboratory results reach the doctor late or not at all. Prescriptions are copied by hand and misread. Allergies are not checked before a drug is given. NHIS claims are submitted late or incomplete, so the hospital loses money it has already earned. Management has no reliable figure for bed occupancy or revenue on any given day.

The system puts all of this on one shared record. When a nurse records vitals or a laboratory technician verifies a result, every role that needs that information sees it at once.

2.4 Operating environment

The staff application runs in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on desktop and tablet, 1280 pixels wide and above. The patient application targets Android and iOS screen sizes from 360 pixels wide. Both are web technologies, so no installation is needed on hospital machines. Internet is required for AI features; the rest of the system works on the local network.

4. How requirements were gathered

We used four methods and cross-checked them against each other.

MethodWho or whatWhat it gave us
InterviewsPractising nurse, pharmacist and hospital accounts officerThe real pain points: missing folders, unreadable prescriptions, late claims.
Process observationOutpatient flow at a district hospitalThe order of stations and where patients wait longest.
Document studyPaper forms, request slips, NHIS claim formsThe exact fields each department needs, which became our data model.
System studyExisting hospital software and published literatureStandard module boundaries and what such systems usually get wrong.

We then wrote user stories per role, grouped them into the ten functional areas in §2.2, removed anything that could not be justified by one of the four sources, and prioritised the rest with MoSCoW.

6. Non-functional requirements

IDQualityRequirement and how it is measured
NFR-01PerformanceAny screen shall render within 2 seconds on a mid-range laptop over hospital Wi-Fi. Measured with browser timing on the ten most-used screens.
NFR-02PerformancePatient search shall return matches within 300 milliseconds for a register of 5,000 patients.
NFR-03UsabilityA nurse shall complete a vitals entry in under 45 seconds without training. Measured in usability testing.
NFR-04UsabilityEvery destructive or clinical action shall give visible confirmation feedback within 1 second.
NFR-05AccessibilityText and background shall meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. Touch targets on mobile shall be at least 44 by 44 pixels.
NFR-06SecurityNo clinical or financial screen shall be reachable without an authenticated session.
NFR-07SecurityAccess shall be enforced by role on every screen, not only hidden from the menu.
NFR-08SecurityPasswords shall never be displayed by default and never written to logs.
NFR-09PrivacyPatient data shall be processed in line with Act 843. A patient shall see only their own record in the mobile application.
NFR-10AuditabilityEvery state-changing action shall be attributable to a named user with a timestamp, and the log shall not be editable from the interface.
NFR-11ReliabilityThe interface shall not lose entered data on browser refresh. Persisted state shall reload in the same condition.
NFR-12ReliabilityFailure of the AI service shall not block any clinical workflow. The system shall show a fallback message and allow manual completion.
NFR-13AvailabilityTarget 99% availability during hospital operating hours.
NFR-14ScalabilityThe design shall support 5,000 patient records, 100 concurrent staff sessions and 12 months of history without redesign.
NFR-15MaintainabilityBusiness logic shall be separated from presentation. Domain data shall be defined in one place per entity.
NFR-16PortabilityThe system shall run on current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari with no installation on client machines.
NFR-17ResponsivenessStaff screens shall be usable from 1280 pixels wide; patient screens from 360 pixels wide.
NFR-18LocalisationCurrency shall display as Ghana Cedis, dates in day-month-year order, and phone numbers in Ghanaian format.
NFR-19SafetyAI output shall be labelled as assistive and shall require human confirmation before it enters the record.
NFR-20Error handlingInvalid input shall be rejected with a specific message naming the field and the rule broken.

7. External interfaces

InterfaceDescription
User interfaceTwo front ends. The staff workspace uses a collapsible sidebar, a top bar with search and notifications, and content panels per role. The patient application uses a five-tab bottom navigation. Both use one type scale and one colour palette, documented in the design document.
AI serviceThe clinical and operations assistants call a large language model. In v2.0 the call is made by the application tier over the cloud provider network, authorised by its compute role, so no key is held by the system and none reaches a browser. Requests carry only the clinical context needed for the task. Failure is handled by NFR-12.
Mobile MoneyVersion 1.0 models the MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash and AT Money request-and-approve flow. A production deployment would replace the modelled step with the provider's collections API.
PrintingPrescriptions, receipts and discharge summaries print through the browser print pipeline on A4 or Letter.
StorageBehind a single persistence layer, which is the point: v1.0 satisfied it with browser storage and v2.0 satisfies it with an HTTP client against a PostgreSQL-backed API, without touching screen logic. The maintenance plan records the migration as carried out.

9. Constraints and assumptions

IDConstraint
CON-01The project must be delivered within one academic semester by a student group, which caps the scope at the modules listed in §2.2.
CON-02No budget for licensed software or commercial API contracts. v2.0 uses the smallest managed database instance and the smallest compute size available, which is enough for the scale in NFR-02 and is a deliberate limit rather than a sizing recommendation.
CON-03Live Mobile Money collection requires a merchant agreement the project cannot obtain, so that step is modelled.
CON-04Real patient data cannot be used. Test data is synthetic but clinically plausible.
CON-05Version 1.0 stores data in browser storage, so records do not currently move between devices. Lifted in v2.0. Records are held in a shared PostgreSQL database, so they follow the patient rather than the device.
CON-06The system must run on existing hospital hardware and bandwidth, so no heavy client-side processing.
CON-07AI features depend on an external service and an internet connection. Still true, and still handled by NFR-12: every feature degrades to a documented fallback message and no clinical action is ever blocked on a model response.
IDAssumption
ASM-01Each staff member has an individual account and does not share credentials.
ASM-02Staff are literate in English and can use a keyboard and mouse or a tablet.
ASM-03Patients own a phone capable of running a modern browser and hold a Mobile Money wallet.
ASM-04The hospital has a local network on which staff workstations reach the application.
ASM-05Clinical staff exercise their own judgement and treat AI output as a suggestion.
ASM-06Departmental workflow follows the sequence observed during requirements gathering.
