1. Before you start

1.1 What you need

A computer or tablet with a web browser, and the address of the system from your IT office. Nothing needs to be installed. Patients need a phone with a browser.

1.2 Signing in

  1. Open the system address in your browser.
  2. Choose your role from the buttons on the login screen. The email field fills in for you.
  3. Enter your password and select Sign In.

The first time you sign in, a short welcome panel explains what your role can do. Read it and select Get started. It will not appear again.

Never share your password. Every action you take is recorded against your name in the audit log. If somebody else works under your login, their mistakes are recorded as yours.

1.3 Finding your way around

Part of the screenWhat it is for
Sidebar, leftYour screens. Only what your role may use is shown. Collapse it with the arrow at the bottom to gain space.
Top barPatient search, notifications, today's date and time, your name and role.
Search shortcutHold Ctrl and press K, or select the ⌘K button. Type a patient name, an MRN or a screen name, then select the result.
Status chipsSmall rounded labels showing state. Each carries a colour and a word, so you can read them in a printout or if you do not see colour well.
Confirmation messagesA dark bar at the bottom right confirms every action that changed something. If you do not see it, the action did not save.

Anything shown in purple with a sparkle mark is AI assistance. Treat it as a suggestion to check, never as a finding to accept unread.

1.4 Signing out

Select the sign-out button at the bottom of the sidebar. Do this whenever you leave a shared workstation, even for a few minutes.

2. Receptionist

Your screens: Front Desk, Register Patient, Appointments.
Your job in the system: get patients into the queue correctly, with the right record attached.

2.1 Register a new patient

  1. Select Register Patient in the sidebar.
  2. Fill in name, age, sex and phone number. These are required.
  3. Set blood group and insurance provider if the patient knows them.
  4. Type allergies separated by commas. Get this right. The system uses it to block dangerous prescriptions later.
  5. Add any chronic conditions such as hypertension or diabetes.
  6. Select Register Patient. The system creates the MRN and shows it to you. Write it on the patient's card.

If a required field is empty the system refuses and names the field. Fill it and try again.

2.2 Check in a patient who has an appointment

  1. Open Front Desk. Today's expected patients are listed.
  2. Find the patient and select Check in. They move to the nurse's triage queue at once.

2.3 Handle a walk-in

Search for the patient in the top bar. If they exist, select Add to queue. If they are new, register them first, then add them. Walk-ins need no appointment record.

2.4 Book an appointment

  1. Open Appointments and select Book.
  2. Choose the doctor, then the date, then a time.
  3. Times shown with a line through them are already taken. You cannot select them, and you should not try to squeeze a patient in beside one. Choose another slot or another day.
  4. Select Confirm. The patient receives a notification.
You can see billing information but not clinical records. This is deliberate. If you open a patient chart, the clinical tabs show a restriction notice instead of the data.

3. Nurse

Your screens: Triage Queue, Wards and Beds, Medication Record.
Your job in the system: record accurate vitals, set urgency, manage beds and drug rounds.

3.1 Triage a patient

  1. Open Triage Queue. Patients checked in at the front desk appear here.
  2. Select Record vitals beside the patient.
  3. Enter blood pressure, temperature, pulse, oxygen saturation and weight.
  4. As you type, a purple panel under the inputs suggests an urgency level based on the numbers you entered.
  5. Check the suggestion against what you can see of the patient. You decide, not the system.
  6. Select Save vitals. The patient moves to the doctor's queue with the urgency attached.
If the suggestion says urgent, do not queue the patient and walk away. Tell the doctor in person as well. The system informs; it does not replace handover.

The system refuses impossible readings, for example a pulse of 400. If it refuses, check whether you mistyped.

3.2 Admit a patient to a bed

  1. Open Wards and Beds. Each ward shows its beds and which are occupied.
  2. Select an empty bed, choose the patient, and confirm.
  3. An occupied bed cannot be selected. The system will not allow two patients in one bed.

3.3 Record medication given

Open Medication Record. Each inpatient shows their due drugs. Tick a drug when you have given it. The tick records your name and the time, which is what an audit or a query from the doctor relies on. Tick it when you give it, not at the end of your shift.

3.4 Discharge a patient

On the ward board, select Discharge on the occupied bed. The bed is freed, the event is written to the patient's record, and a discharge summary appears under their documents automatically.

4. Doctor

Your screens: Dashboard, Consultation, My Orders, Patients, Patient Chart.
Your job in the system: document the encounter and place every order from one screen.

4.1 Your dashboard

Your waiting patients are listed with their triage urgency. Above them, a red banner appears if a laboratory result has come back critical for one of your patients. Select Open chart on that banner and deal with it before anything else.

4.2 Conduct a consultation

  1. Select Start consultation beside a patient in your queue.
  2. The left panel shows history, allergies, chronic conditions and the vitals the nurse just recorded. Read the allergy line before you prescribe anything.
  3. Type the chief complaint.
  4. Record your diagnosis and clinical notes.
  5. Stage your orders in the right panel. Nothing leaves the room until you sign.
  6. Select Sign and complete.

The system will not let you sign without a diagnosis. This is intentional. An encounter with no diagnosis is not a record anybody can use later.

4.3 Staging orders

Order typeHow to add it
Laboratory testChoose the test, set routine or STAT, select Add. STAT sorts to the top of the laboratory worklist.
ImagingChoose modality and body region, select Add.
PrescriptionChoose the drug, set dose, frequency and duration, select Add Rx.
AdmissionChoose the ward and select Admit. The nurse assigns the specific bed.
ReferralChoose the specialty and select Add. The patient joins that specialist's queue.
Follow-upChoose an interval such as two weeks. The appointment is booked for you when you sign.

4.4 When a prescription is blocked

If the drug conflicts with a recorded allergy or with something the patient is already taking, the system refuses to stage it and shows a red box naming the conflict. Read the reason. Choose an alternative. The block is there because it is the last check before the drug reaches a pharmacist who cannot see your patient.

A blocked prescription cannot be forced through from this screen. If you judge the drug clinically necessary despite the conflict, that decision must be documented and handled by the pharmacist directly, not worked around in the system.

4.5 Using AI assistance

Select Draft with AI once you have entered a complaint. The system reads the complaint, the vitals and the patient's history, and proposes a diagnosis and a plan. Both fields become editable text you can correct or delete.

The draft is a starting point, not a clinical opinion. You are responsible for what you sign. If the service is unavailable the system says so and you write the note yourself; nothing else in the consultation is affected.

4.6 Reading the patient chart

5. Laboratory technician

Your screens: Sample Worklist, Result Entry.
Your job in the system: move each test through its stages and verify results before release.

5.1 Working the worklist

Orders arrive from doctors. STAT orders sit at the top and are marked. Work each test through four stages in order:

  1. Collect sample. Select it once the sample is drawn.
  2. Start processing. Select it when the sample is on the analyser.
  3. Enter result. Type the value. The reference range is shown beside the field.
  4. Verify. Check the value against the range, then select Verify.

The system offers only the next stage, so you cannot skip ahead. This keeps the record honest about where a sample actually is.

Verification is the release point. Nothing reaches the doctor or the patient until you verify. If a result is out of range enough to be flagged critical, verifying it sends a red alert straight to the ordering doctor. Verify carefully; do not verify a result you have not read.

6. Radiologist

Your screens: Imaging Worklist, Report Entry.
Your job in the system: report scans so findings reach the chart.
  1. Open Imaging Worklist. Orders are grouped by modality with priority flags shown.
  2. Select the scan you have performed and mark it scanned.
  3. Select Report, type your findings, and submit.

Your report attaches to the patient chart under Results and appears in their documents. The charge is added to their invoice automatically.

7. Pharmacist

Your screens: Prescriptions, Inventory.
Your job in the system: dispense correctly and keep stock accurate.

7.1 Dispense a prescription

  1. Open Prescriptions. Pending items are listed with drug, dose, frequency, duration and the prescribing doctor.
  2. Check the patient's identity against the record before handing anything over.
  3. Select the printer icon if the patient needs a printed slip.
  4. Select Dispense. Stock reduces automatically and the item is marked dispensed.

You do not need to adjust stock by hand. Doing so as well would double count.

Prescriptions that reached you have already passed the allergy and interaction checks at the point of prescribing. That does not remove your professional check. If something looks wrong, contact the prescriber. The system's checks cover recorded allergies and a known set of interactions, not every possible clinical concern.

7.2 Watch your inventory

Open Inventory. Items below reorder level are flagged low stock, and items approaching expiry are flagged too. Check this daily and raise your order before an item runs out, not after.

8. Cashier

Your screens: Invoices, Insurance Claims.
Your job in the system: collect what is owed and get claims paid.

8.1 Understanding an invoice

Invoices build themselves. Every consultation, test, scan and drug adds its own line as it happens, so you do not assemble a bill by hand. Each invoice shows its lines, total, amount paid and a status of unpaid, part paid or paid.

8.2 Take a cash payment

Find the invoice, select Record payment, and confirm. Select the printer icon for a receipt.

8.3 Take a Mobile Money payment

  1. Select MoMo on the invoice row.
  2. Choose the provider: MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash or AT Money.
  3. Enter or confirm the patient's wallet number.
  4. Select Send payment request. The patient receives a prompt on their phone.
  5. When they have entered their PIN, select Patient has approved.
  6. Select Record payment. The invoice is settled and the audit entry names the provider.
Do not select Record payment until you have seen or been told the payment went through on the patient's phone. The button records money as received.

8.4 Insurance claims

Each insured invoice carries a claim. Move it along as the insurer responds: submitted, then authorised, then paid. Select the sparkle button on a claim to have a justification drafted for you. Read it, correct anything that is not accurate for this patient, and only then send it.

You can see billing and claims but not clinical records. If a claim needs clinical detail you do not have, ask the treating clinician rather than looking for a way around the restriction.

9. Administrator

Your screens: Dashboard, Staff, Audit Log.
Your job in the system: oversight, accountability and answering questions about the hospital.

9.1 Dashboard

Patient count, appointments today, revenue, bed occupancy and department load. Below them, Live Activity streams actions from every role as they happen.

9.2 Ask the operations assistant

Type a question in plain words, for example "how many beds are free in the general ward" or "what revenue is outstanding", and select Ask. The assistant answers from current system data only. If it cannot reach the service, it says so rather than guessing.

9.3 Audit log

Every action by every user, with actor, action, target and time. The log cannot be edited or deleted from anywhere in the system, including your own account. That is what makes it worth having.

10. Patients using the mobile application

Patients use the mobile application only. A desktop patient portal exists in the system for use at a hospital kiosk or reception terminal, and it mirrors the mobile screens exactly: dashboard, booking, records, bills and profile. Everything in this section applies to both. The instructions are written for the phone because that is where patients will use it.

10.1 First time

Open the application address on your phone. Four short screens explain what you can do. Tap Next through them, or Skip. Then sign in with the email or phone number you gave at the hospital, and your password. You stay signed in until you sign out.

10.2 Home

Your next appointment sits at the top. Below it, four buttons for booking a visit, checking test results, viewing bills and calling the emergency line. Your active medications are listed at the bottom with their status.

10.3 Book an appointment

  1. Tap Book at the bottom, or Book visit on the home screen.
  2. Choose a doctor, then a date, then a time.
  3. Times with a line through them are taken. Pick another.
  4. Tap Confirm booking. You will get a reminder before the appointment.

10.4 Your records

Tap Records. Three tabs: Results, Meds and Bills. Results appear only after the laboratory has verified them, so a test you did this morning may not be there yet. Tap "What does this mean?" on any result for an explanation in plain words.

The explanation helps you understand your result. It is not medical advice and it is not a diagnosis. Discuss anything that concerns you with your doctor.

10.5 Pay a bill with Mobile Money

  1. Tap Records, then Bills.
  2. Tap Pay now on the unpaid bill.
  3. Choose your provider and check your MoMo number.
  4. Tap Send payment request.
  5. Enter your MoMo PIN on the prompt that appears on your phone.
  6. You will see a receipt with a reference number. Keep the reference.

10.6 The symptom checker

Tap Symptom checker on the home screen. Describe how you feel in your own words. It will suggest which kind of doctor to see and how soon, and offer to open booking.

This is not a diagnosis and it cannot examine you. If you are seriously unwell, in severe pain, bleeding heavily, struggling to breathe or losing consciousness, do not use the application. Call the emergency line or go to the hospital now.

11. Common problems

What you seeWhat to do
Nothing happened when I selected a buttonLook for the confirmation bar at the bottom right. If it did not appear, the action did not save. Try again. If it still fails, note what you were doing and tell IT.
A slot I want is crossed outAnother patient holds it. Choose a different time or day. Do not overbook.
My prescription was blockedRead the red message. It names the allergy or interaction. Choose a different drug.
The AI feature says it is unavailableThe internet connection or the service is down. Everything else keeps working. Write the note or the justification yourself.
A result I expect is not in the chartThe laboratory has not verified it yet. Results release on verification, not on entry.
A clinical tab shows a lock noticeYour role does not have clinical access. This is correct, not a fault. Ask a clinician.
I cannot find a patient by nameCheck the spelling, or search by MRN instead. If they are genuinely absent, they were never registered.
I want to start the demonstration data overSelect Reset demo data on the login screen. This clears everything and restores the original set. Do not do this on a live system.

12. Rules for everybody

  1. One login per person. Never work under someone else's account.
  2. Sign out when you leave a shared machine.
  3. Record allergies accurately at registration. The safety checks depend on that field being right.
  4. Tick medications when you give them, not later from memory.
  5. Verify a laboratory result only after reading it. Verification releases it to the doctor and the patient.
  6. Read AI suggestions before accepting them. You are accountable for what you sign, not the system.
  7. A blocked action is protecting a patient. Find out why before looking for a way around it.
  8. Look for the confirmation message. No message means nothing saved.
